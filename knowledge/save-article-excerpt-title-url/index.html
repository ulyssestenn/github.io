How to Save an Article Excerpt and Keep the Page Title and URL With It

The excerpt is what you want at the moment of reading. The title and URL are what make it understandable and usable again later.

Donald

Copying a paragraph from an article is easy. The failure usually happens a few weeks later, when the paragraph is sitting in a notes file with no clear answer to three basic questions: where did this come from, what was the article, and how do I get back to it?

The text survived. Its provenance did not.

Keep Clip is designed around Android sharing, so an excerpt can be captured together with the surrounding source information when the app or browser provides it.

Select the passage before you share

In a browser or reading app, select the excerpt you actually want to keep and use the Android Share command. Choose Keep Clip.

That is better than copying the whole article into a notes app and trimming it later. Capture is most useful when it preserves the small piece that earned your attention in the first place.

Keep Clip separates the excerpt from the page information

Different Android apps format shared text differently. A browser may send selected text followed by a page title and URL. Another app may send only a title and link. Some apps provide additional source information.

Keep Clip's extraction logic tries to separate those pieces. The selected text can remain the clip itself while the page title and source URL are stored in their own fields. It also attempts to identify the source app.

This matters because a URL embedded at the bottom of a long text block is technically preserved but structurally weak. A dedicated source field is easier to recognize, search, open, and export later.

Check the title and URL before saving

Metadata extraction is necessarily best effort because Android apps do not all share content the same way. Some shares omit the URL. Some provide an incomplete title. Some expose less source information than others.

Before saving an important excerpt, glance at the title and URL fields. If the source arrived correctly, do nothing. If something essential is missing, add it while the article is still open and easy to identify.

That small check is cheaper than trying to reconstruct provenance six months later.

Add a note only when the excerpt will not explain itself

An article excerpt often preserves what the author said but not why you cared about it. If that reason is likely to be obvious later, no note is necessary.

If the passage matters because it supports a specific argument, contradicts another source, belongs in a project, or suggests a question to pursue, add one short note. The note should preserve the decision or connection that is not already present in the source text.

Title and URL make later search much stronger

Keep Clip searches across stored fields, not just the main excerpt. That means you may be able to recover a clip by a word from the article title, a source domain, an author, a tag, or language from the passage itself.

The difference is practical. A bare quotation depends on remembering the quotation. A quotation with title and URL can be found from several directions and can lead back to the larger source.

A URL is provenance, not preservation

Keeping the URL does not guarantee that the webpage will remain unchanged or available forever. Sites move, articles are revised, paywalls change, and pages disappear.

For ordinary reading and research capture, the link is still valuable because it records where the excerpt came from and often provides a path back. For material that must remain permanently recoverable, use a proper archival workflow in addition to the clip.

The useful minimum is small

A strong article clip usually needs only four things: the excerpt, the page title, the URL, and perhaps one sentence about why it mattered.

That is enough context to make the passage useful again without turning every interesting paragraph into a miniature cataloging exercise.

The tool used in this workflow

Keep Clip

Keep Clip receives text through Android's Share menu and can separate selected content from page titles and URLs, detect the source app, and store title, URL, author, notes, tags, and other context alongside the excerpt. Clips remain searchable and can be exported in portable formats.
