How to Turn an Old Android Tablet Into a Dedicated E-Reader

An older tablet can become a better reading device when it stops trying to be a general-purpose tablet. Give it one job, remove interruptions, and keep the library available offline.

Donald

An old Android tablet may be unpleasant for modern web browsing and still be perfectly adequate for books. Text does not ask much of a processor. What usually makes the device feel unsuitable for reading is everything surrounding the book: slow browser tabs, notification traffic, half a dozen signed-in services, and a home screen designed for general-purpose use.

Repurposing the tablet is mostly a matter of deciding what it no longer needs to do.

First, make sure the hardware is still worth using

A dedicated reader still needs a decent screen, a reliable charging port, enough battery for a reading session, and enough storage for the books you want on it. A weak processor is rarely the decisive problem. A swollen battery, badly damaged display, or unreliable charging port is.

Security support matters too. If the tablet no longer receives updates, keep its job narrow. There is little reason for an obsolete reading appliance to hold sensitive accounts, email, payment information, or a browser full of authenticated sessions. Books can remain useful on hardware that you would no longer trust as your everyday computer.

Make opening a book the easiest thing the tablet can do

Remove or disable apps that regularly compete for attention, especially social feeds, games, shopping apps, and noisy news notifications. Turn off most system notifications. Put the reader where the thumb naturally lands on the home screen. Utilities can stay, but they do not need to occupy the same visual priority as the device's new purpose.

This is a small design intervention, not a theory of human attention. A determined person can still open a browser. The advantage is simply that a reading session no longer begins with six invitations to do something else.

Once the tablet is quiet, set up the reader around the books you actually have. Commercial purchases may require the corresponding storefront app. EPUB files and public-domain books give you more freedom to choose a reader for typography, search, bookmarks, notes, and reliable offline access rather than for the store attached to it.

Give it a real offline shelf

A dedicated reader is more useful when the next book is already there. Download several books before a trip, an outage, or a long afternoon away from Wi-Fi. Text-heavy EPUB files are small enough that an older tablet can usually hold far more books than anyone needs in an immediate queue.

Then adjust the physical reading experience rather than accepting the device's old settings by default:

  • use a text size that remains comfortable after an hour, not one chosen to maximize words per page;
  • choose a light, warm, or dark theme according to the room and your eyes;
  • set screen timeout so it is not constantly interrupting a slow page;
  • use airplane mode when the books are downloaded and connectivity adds nothing; and
  • consider a simple stand if a larger tablet will mostly live beside a chair, bed, or desk.

The resulting device does not need to imitate a new dedicated e-reader in every respect. If it wakes reliably, opens the current book, works without a network, and stays out of the way while you read, an otherwise obsolete tablet can still have a very good job.

A relevant Ulix tool

Guten

Guten is a free Android reader for the Project Gutenberg catalog. It can search and import public-domain books, keep them available offline, and supports bookmarks, highlights, notes, text search, and export. On an old Android tablet, it is one way to create a large classics library without making a commercial bookstore the center of the device.
