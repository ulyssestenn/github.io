# Project Gutenberg books for very early English readers

Research for [Guten's existing English-learning landing page](./index.html). Compiled 2026-10-26.

## Editorial conclusion

The current landing page's "Start here" shelf begins with *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*, *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*, *The Jungle Book*, *The Secret Garden*, and *The Call of the Wild*. These are engaging *later* reads, but **not suitable entry points for A0/A1 English-language learners**. Add a clearly labelled "Your first English books" shelf, or a companion page, and preserve the ambitious classics farther down the reading journey.

**Do not equate primary-school reading grade with CEFR proficiency.** The tiers below are human editorial estimates made from the intended audience, sentence style, recurring vocabulary, length, and the available original excerpts. They have **not** been CEFR-tested. Project Gutenberg's automated "reading ease" score is also not a CEFR score. Adult beginners may read primer language easily but dislike its juvenile subject matter; keep short, entertaining stories beside primers.

A suggested path:
1. **First words / A0–early A1:** primers, one-page episodes, repetitive simple patterns.
2. **First stories / later A1–A2 (with occasional help):** illustrated stories with short plots and familiar characters; some originals contain surprisingly advanced or archaic vocabulary.
3. **Next books / A2–B1:** short chapter books and story collections, not for absolute beginners.

## What I recommend actually featuring on the landing page

**Do not dump the entire primer inventory into the hero shelf.** It risks making Guten look like an elementary school textbook app. Mix confidence-building reads with appealing, complete stories:

- *For your very first English sentences:* **The Summers Readers: Primer** (#68453), **McGuffey's Eclectic Primer** (#14642).
- *When you know a few sentences:* **A First Reader** (#65323), **Kittens and Cats** (#61852).
- *Finish a whole story:* **Miss Moppet** (#14848), **The Three Little Pigs** (#18155), **Peter Rabbit** (#14838, with help).
- *Read about adventures, myths and history:* **A Primary Reader** (#7841), **Fifty Famous Stories Retold** (#18442).
- *Your first bigger book:* **The Box-Car Children** (#42796, later A2/B1).

For real usability, label the offerings by ability or reading experience, not by grade-school age. A good sequence could be **"First English words" → "Your first complete story" → "Stories you can finish in a day" → "Your first chapter book" → "The classics"**. Book cards should state when illustrations, repeated language, archaic vocabulary, or adult supervision/lookup help are expected.

The landing page currently says "You've learned English. Now read its classics." This is valid for its existing audience, but very early ESL readers need more reassurance and a distinct entry point such as **"Your first English book is waiting."**

## Editorial / technical verification still needed before production

- **Illustration fidelity:** Project Gutenberg hosts illustrated source editions for many titles, but test whether Guten's **specific import path and EPUB renderer** preserve illustrations; no app-level verification was performed here.
- **Opening-text sanity check:** Read the first chapter or several pages in the precise Gutenberg edition before marking a title "beginner." Short does not equal linguistically simple; the first paragraphs of *The Little Red Hen* and *Three Little Pigs* are good counterexamples.
- **Audience sensitivity:** These are historical books. Some feature antiquated language, behavior, stereotypes, or moral frameworks; inspect before promoting particular passages.
- **Copyright:** Gutenberg labels the cited editions public domain **in the United States**. Distribution and reader permissions differ in other countries; retain the existing site notice and Gutenberg attribution.
- **CEFR:** These labels are editorial estimates only; do not claim an objective CEFR score, especially for a whole anthology with many difficulty levels.
- **Scope:** This is a curated research shortlist, not an implementation, a formal readability validation, or an assertion that the app's built-in search currently indexes every item.
