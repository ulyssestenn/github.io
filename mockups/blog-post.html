Your notes should outlive your apps

Every Ulix app exports to plain, portable formats — Markdown, CSV, TXT. That's not a feature on a checklist. It's the contract.

By Bethany Hunt · Founder, Ulix

Somewhere on an old hard drive, you probably have notes trapped in a format only one dead program could read. We've all paid that tax once. The apps we build start from the promise that you'll never pay it again.

When you highlight a passage in Guten, save a quote with Keep Clip, or log a symptom in Track Analysis, that record belongs to you — not to us, and not to the app. It lives on your device, and it leaves whenever you ask it to, in a format any text editor, spreadsheet, or LLM can read.

The test every feature has to pass

Before anything ships, it answers one question:

If Ulix disappeared tomorrow, would your data still be useful? If the answer is no, the feature doesn't ship.

That rule sounds simple, but it quietly decides a lot:

  • Plain formats only. Markdown, CSV, TXT, RTF, HTML — never a proprietary blob with our logo on it.
  • Export is a first-class screen, not a buried setting. If it takes more than two taps, it's a bug.
  • No accounts means there's no server-side copy to lose, leak, or hold hostage.
  • Structure survives the trip. Tags, dates, and sources ride along with the text.

What an export actually looks like

Here's a Keep Clip export, verbatim. Notice there's nothing to decode — it's just Markdown you could paste into Obsidian, a Git repo, or a prompt:

# The Odyssey — Emily Wilson translation
> "Tell me about a complicated man."
Saved: 2026-07-14 · Tags: openers, translation
Source: Guten · Book I, line 1

Why we're writing this down

"Own the data" is easy to say and cheap to print on a landing page. Publishing the rule — and the test behind it — makes it something you can hold us to. When a future release note claims a new export format, this essay is the standard it gets measured against.

Your reading, your clippings, your logs: they're a record of your attention. Records are meant to outlast their tools. Ours are built to.
